2. Literature-review content starter
2.1 Accountability and assurance

Responsible-AI research increasingly argues that assurance should span the complete AI lifecycle rather than rely on isolated post-deployment audits. Raji et al. propose an end-to-end internal algorithmic auditing framework in which each development stage creates documentation contributing to an overall audit record. TrustLLM expands the technical perspective by organizing LLM trustworthiness across dimensions including truthfulness, safety, fairness, robustness, privacy and ethics. Together, these works establish that trustworthy AI requires both organizational accountability and measurable technical evaluation.

P2 builds on this direction by connecting ethical impact assessment, TEVV results, human approval, evidence recording and final release within one state-controlled workflow. Its research question is not whether governance documentation is useful, but whether governance requirements can be transformed into enforceable and independently verifiable system states.

2.2 RAG and automated evaluation

RAGAs and ARES demonstrate that retrieval-augmented systems can be evaluated through dimensions such as context relevance, answer faithfulness and answer relevance. RAGAs focuses on reference-free automated metrics, while ARES employs synthetic data and trained judges alongside statistical estimation.

These frameworks provide important baselines, but P2 must examine the reliability of automated judges for Arabic policy and governance contexts. P2 therefore proposes a hybrid methodology combining human-labelled evidence, retrieval metrics, Arabic NLI, confidence thresholds and explicit abstention.

2.3 Hallucination and citation verification

Ji et al. provide a broad taxonomy of hallucination definitions, evaluation methods and mitigation strategies. FActScore evaluates long-form generation by decomposing it into atomic facts and checking each fact against a knowledge source. ALCE evaluates whether generated claims are adequately and correctly supported by citations. SelfCheckGPT represents a different family of methods based on consistency across repeated generations, while semantic entropy estimates uncertainty over meanings.

P2 adopts evidence-based claim verification because consistency alone does not demonstrate that an AI-generated claim agrees with an authoritative source. Its intended contribution is to combine atomic claims, hybrid evidence retrieval, Arabic NLI, evidence sufficiency and traceable evidence references.

2.4 Arabic retrieval, dialects and bias

MIRACL provides multilingual information-retrieval benchmarks with native-speaker relevance judgements, including Arabic. AraDiCE examines Arabic dialectal and cultural capability, including Egyptian contexts. BBQ provides a controlled approach to measuring stereotypical behaviour, although its English and US setting limits direct conclusions about Egyptian Arabic. ArGAN contributes an Arabic MSA bias benchmark across gender, ability and nationality.

These works demonstrate that general Arabic performance does not guarantee reliable dialectal or culturally appropriate behaviour. P2 therefore proposes a Dialect Robustness Score calibrated against human ratings, with explicit analysis for Egyptian Arabic, MSA and Arabic-English code-switching.

2.5 Human validation

SummEval demonstrates the importance of comparing automatic evaluation metrics against expert and crowdworker judgements. P2 applies this principle to DRS by comparing alternative weighting approaches against human overall ratings and measuring correlation, error and inter-rater reliability.

SummEval is a methodological reference only. Its task-specific scores or weights must not be transferred directly to P2.

2.6 Evidence integrity

Crosby and Wallach examine cryptographic structures for efficient tamper-evident logging. RFC 9162 specifies Merkle inclusion and consistency proofs in Certificate Transparency. These sources support P2’s evidence-integrity design, particularly hash chaining, Merkle batching and independent proof verification.

Cryptographic integrity does not prove that input evidence is truthful. It proves whether committed evidence was changed, deleted, reordered or incorrectly included after recording. P2 must keep this threat-model boundary explicit.

Proposed research gap

Existing work separately addresses algorithmic auditing, trustworthiness evaluation, RAG assessment, factuality, Arabic dialect capability, bias and tamper-evident logging. P2 investigates their integration into an Egypt-focused assurance workflow where an AI result cannot become final until required assessment, human review, evidence recording, cryptographic proof generation and independent acknowledgement are completed.
